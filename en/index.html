Cross-domain information analysis

Open data, layer by layer, to an answer a municipality can use.

Municipal issues cut across policy domains. The answer is often already in public registers, spread over sources that nobody puts side by side. That is where the work begins.

Look differently. Discover connections. Make meaning visible.

Every register tells its own story. Something new emerges in the connections between them. The Open Data Atelier is the place to make those connections. What becomes visible is also what is missing, what deviates and what turns out unexpectedly. That is often exactly where the next question begins.

Transparency and dialogue are the starting points. Transparency: source, calculation rule and uncertainty are open, so anyone can check how an outcome came about. Dialogue: an instrument does not say whether something is good, enough or fairly distributed. You have that conversation yourself, with colleagues, the council and residents. Anyone who does not recognise something, or knows more, makes the picture better.

Sometimes looking differently is enough to see what was there all along.

Working method

Three principles

Traceable

Every outcome shows how it came about: which source, which threshold, which exception. Anyone who wants to check can do so per object.

Uncertainty made visible

Where open data falls short, the page says so. An honest category "uncertain" is more useful than a forced yes or no; it is a work list straight away.

The code goes to the data

Sensitive data stay where they belong. Local knowledge is added in the municipality's own environment, not sent anywhere.

Instruments · Municipal view

From open data to a usable instrument, for every municipality

A report records what was seen. A dashboard follows fixed figures over time. An instrument is made to investigate a question: you choose where to look, a municipality, a district, a year, a building. It calculates on the spot and shows where the answer comes from. The instruments themselves are in Dutch: they are built for the 342 Dutch municipalities and their open data.

Grants by municipality

A municipality awards grants (subsidies) for sport and culture, welfare, monuments, insulation. The rules for them are spread over dozens of documents. This page brings them together per municipality: the general grants bye-law as the legal basis, the policy rules, the decisions on grant ceilings and all separate grant schemes listed as current in the local regulations, each with a link to the text. What can be recognised by its name as possibly expired is set apart. The amount the municipality books for grants and contributions comes from the Iv3 mirror. The number of schemes is not a measure: municipalities bundle them differently, and there is no ranking. Amounts per scheme and recipients are not in open data. For policy officers, grant coordinators, council members and anyone looking for a grant.

How it works
